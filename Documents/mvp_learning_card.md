## [Hypothesis 1: Students want to filter courses by workload, difficulty, assessment types, and relevance](./mvp_test_card.md#hypothesis-1-students-want-to-filter-courses-by-workload-difficulty-assessment-types-and-relevance)

**HYPOTHESIS**

We believed that: 
UQ students want to compare and filter courses by workload, difficulty, assessment types, relevance, or similar factors when choosing electives.

**OBSERVATION**

We observed:
We found that roughly 18 out of 23 students mentioned workload, difficulty, assessment style, course level, semester availability, or how well the course fit their other subjects when describing how they choose electives.

This means the hypothesis is accepted, since the result is above the 17 out of 23 acceptance bar we would use for a 7/10 threshold.

**LEARNING AND INSIGHTS**

From that we learnt that:
Course comparison is not just about difficulty. Students want a way to compare several practical trade-offs at once, especially workload, assessment makeup, and how a subject fits with the rest of their degree and timetable. The most useful first version should therefore keep the filter set simple, but still flexible enough to reflect different student priorities.

**DECISIONS AND ACTIONS**

Therefore we will:
Keep the comparison tool focused on a small set of high-signal filters first, then expand to semester availability, course level, and timetable clash checks if the first prototype tests well.

## [Hypothesis 2: Students want a repository of course summaries with prerequisites, assessments, and workload](./mvp_test_card.md#hypothesis-2-students-want-a-repository-of-course-summaries-with-prerequisites-assessments-and-workload)

**HYPOTHESIS**

We believed that:
UQ students want a clear repository of course summaries that includes prerequisites, assessments, and workload information before enrolling.

**OBSERVATION**

We observed:
We found that roughly 20 out of 23 students mentioned prerequisites, assessment breakdowns, workload, or course content as information they look for before enrolling.

This means the hypothesis is accepted, since the result is above the 14 out of 23 acceptance bar we would use for a 6/10 threshold.

**LEARNING AND INSIGHTS**

From that we learnt that:
Students want more than a course description. They want a readable summary that tells them what they will learn, how they will be assessed, how hard the course is likely to feel, and whether it is worth taking. A summary page should therefore reduce the need to search across course profiles, study plans, and external discussions.

**DECISIONS AND ACTIONS**

Therefore we will:
Design the summary view so that the most useful information appears in one place, with clear sections for course purpose, topics covered, assessments, workload, and prerequisites.

## [Hypothesis 3: Students want a guided semester-by-semester course planner](./mvp_test_card.md#hypothesis-3-students-want-a-guided-semester-by-semester-course-planner)

**HYPOTHESIS**

We believed that:
UQ students want a guided semester-by-semester planner to help them plan courses across future semesters.

**OBSERVATION**

We observed:
We found that roughly 15 out of 23 students described needing help planning courses across semesters, especially when checking study plans, semester availability, or prerequisite order.

This means the hypothesis is accepted, since the result is above the 12 out of 23 acceptance bar we would use for a 5/10 threshold.

**LEARNING AND INSIGHTS**

From that we learnt that:
Students do want help planning across semesters, but they want more than a static list. They want a planner that shows what fits next, what is blocked by prerequisites, and what semester each course is offered in. A useful planner should also make it easy to see if changing one course affects the rest of the degree plan.

**DECISIONS AND ACTIONS**

Therefore we will:
Prioritise a planner that shows semester availability, prerequisite chains, and degree progress in a visual way so students can see the consequences of their choices earlier.

## [Hypothesis 4: Students want a requirement-matching and prerequisite-checking tool](./mvp_test_card.md#hypothesis-4-students-want-a-requirement-matching-and-prerequisite-checking-tool)

**HYPOTHESIS**

We believed that:
UQ students want a tool that checks degree fit, prerequisite chains, and program requirements.

**OBSERVATION**

We observed:
We found that roughly 18 out of 23 students described difficulty checking degree fit, prerequisite chains, or program requirements.

This means the hypothesis is accepted, since the result is above the 17 out of 23 acceptance bar we would use for a 7/10 threshold.

**LEARNING AND INSIGHTS**

From that we learnt that:
The biggest pain point is not only whether a course is interesting, but whether it is actually allowed. Students want the system to clearly say if they are eligible, what prerequisite is missing, and how a course fits into the wider degree path. A visual eligibility state would be more useful than making users infer the answer from dense text.

**DECISIONS AND ACTIONS**

Therefore we will:
Design the prerequisite feature as a clear eligibility checker with a simple visual status and a prerequisite flow rather than leaving students to piece it together manually.

## [Hypothesis 5: Students want course recommendations or peer feedback to help them choose](./mvp_test_card.md#hypothesis-5-students-want-course-recommendations-or-peer-feedback-to-help-them-choose)

**HYPOTHESIS**

We believed that:
UQ students want course recommendations or peer feedback to help them decide whether to take a course.

**OBSERVATION**

We observed:
We found that roughly 19 out of 23 students mentioned using friends, seniors, Reddit, forums, or online discussion to judge what a course is really like.

This means the hypothesis is accepted, since the result is above the 14 out of 23 acceptance bar we would use for a 6/10 threshold.

**LEARNING AND INSIGHTS**

From that we learnt that:
Students trust peer feedback when it is specific enough to reveal what the course actually feels like. General praise is not as useful as practical detail about workload, assessment style, and how hard the course is to manage alongside other subjects.

**DECISIONS AND ACTIONS**

Therefore we will:
Treat peer feedback as a supporting feature that surfaces concise, practical student experience rather than as a general review feed.
