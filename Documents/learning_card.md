## [Hypothesis 1: Choosing Courses is confusing:](./test_card.md#hypothesis-1-choosing-courses-is-confusing)
**HYPOTHESIS**

We Believed that: 
UQ Students find the process of choosing courses confusing or time-consuming.

**OBSERVATION**

We observed:
We found that roughly 7/10 students found the process of choosing courses either difficult or time consuming this semester. Students reported difficulties with things such as [elective choice](../Interviews/Iteration_1/Jinhan/Jinhan_20260319.md) and [finding course information](../Interviews/Iteration_1/Laura/Laura_2026_03_26_3.md) in particular.

**LEARNING AND INSIGHTS**

From that we learnt that:
Students seem to struggle with the amount of electives offered at UQ and there is no easy way of viewing all elective options easily. Furthermore for students studying more non-standard degrees and majors (dual degrees etc.) this becomes a problem for core courses. We also found that these problems were [exacerbated for exchange students](../Interviews/Iteration_1/Kezia/Kezia_20260325_3.md) as they not only had to balance the requirements and prequesites of UQ but also with their home university. We also found that UQ students in general found resources for course selection [unintuitive to access](../Interviews/Iteration_1/Laura/Laura_2026_03_26_4.md).

In terms of time taken we found that UQ students ended up taking a pretty variable amount of time to finalise courses, with some taking as few as [a few hours](../Interviews/Iteration_1/Ranvir/Inverview4.md) and some taking as long as [a month](../Interviews/Iteration_1/Kayla/Kayla_20260325_1.md) in some cases. This lead us to conclude that while the majority of interviewees reported finding course selection difficult or time consuming, the extent to which time was consumed (the amount of time taken for course selection) was much more variable than how much and how students found the process difficult. 

**DECISIONS AND ACTIONS**

Therefore we will:
Based on these findings we will be focusing on specifically on the identified pain points of elective choice and the difficulty students found in finding course information during our product development. If we can specifically focus on these two areas of difficulty identified in our research we believe we can create a valuable product for UQ students. Furthermore while we found the pain points and extent of the problem with regards to course selection being time consuming to be too variable to properly target we hope that by addressing the specific difficulties we identified we will be able to alleviate some of the time consuming aspects of course selection as a byproduct of making the process easier.

## [Hypothesis 2: Students use a variety of sources from a vareity of places to help course selection:](./test_card.md#hypothesis-2-students-use-a-variety-of-sources-from-a-vareity-of-places-to-help-course-selection)

**HYPOTHESIS**

We Believed that: 
UQ students use multiple scattered sources (course profiles, forums, friends, etc.) when choosing courses.


**OBSERVATION**

We observed:
We found that roughly two thirds (6.7/10) of students interviewed used a variety of different resources when choosing their courses. We also found that the majority of the students surveyed reported asking for [peer advice or checking online](../Interviews/Iteration_1/Kayla/Kayla_20260319_1.md) for qualitative assessment of their prospective courses. While we found that most students used UQ resources (course profiles, study plans etc.) for their course selection, many also used external resources particularly when it came to getting peer feedback on courses.

**LEARNING AND INSIGHTS**

From that we learnt that:
The resources students use seems to be correlated somewhat with their goals when choosing courses as well as with how easy they found the process (less resources) or how difficult they found it (more resources). Students that reported a simple straightforward course selection process tended to be [less inclined](../Interviews/Iteration_1/Kezia/Kezia_20260319_1.md) to want to use a variety of resources as it seemed like they were approaching the process from a far more [utilitarian perspective](../Interviews/Iteration_1/Laura/Laura_2026_03_26_4.md). Furthermore it seemed like as students progressed into the later years of their degrees they were much more focused on what they wanted to get from courses and had also refined the process of course selection to the extent that they used a smaller pool of resources and information.

When it came to peer feedback on courses this was by far the most common reason students reported for using a [variety of sources](../Interviews/Iteration_1/Jinhan/Jinhan_20260321.md) when choosing courses. Most commonly this was looking up the opinions and [advice of other students online](../Interviews/Iteration_1/Laura/Laura_2026_03_26_1.md) with regards to the courses they were considering taking. In addition to this, students often cited learning about how [difficult a course and its assessments are](../Interviews/Iteration_1/Jinhan/Jinhan_20260318.md) as a reason for seeking out peer feedback.

**DECISIONS AND ACTIONS**

Therefore we will:
As a result of these findings we will likely focus on first year students as our main customers for this project as we found they are the most likely to be using the kind of resource we hope to create and will therefore get the most value out of it. As these students are using the greatest variety of information, some attempt to centralise the information with regards to course selection we feel would be very useful as we [also found when investigating our other hypothesis.](./learning_card.md#hypothesis-1-choosing-courses-is-confusing) Furthermore based on how important it seemed to the students being interviewed, some mechanism for viewing peer feedback within our product could be something useful to explore as that was shown to be one of the main reasons students explored external resources for course selection.
