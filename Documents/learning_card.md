## [Hypothesis 1: Choosing Courses is confusing:](./test_card.md#hypothesis-1-choosing-courses-is-confusing)
**HYPOTHESIS**
We Believed that: 
UQ Students find the process of choosing courses confusing or time-consuming.
**OBSERVATION**
We observed:
We found that roughly 7/10 students found the process of choosing courses either difficult or time consuming this semester. Students reported difficulties with things such as [elective choice](../Interviews/Iteration_1/Jinhan/Jinhan_20260319.md) and [finding course information](../Interviews/Iteration_1/Laura/Laura_2026_03_26_3.md) in particular.
**LEARNING AND INSIGHTS**
From that we learnt that:
Students seem to struggle with the amount of electives offered at UQ and there is no easy way of viewing all elective options easily. Furthermore for students studying more non-standard degrees and majors (dual degrees etc.) this becomes a problem for core courses. We also found that these problems were [exacerbated for exchange students](../Interviews/Iteration_1/Kezia/Kezia_20260325_3.md) as they not only had to balance the requirements and prequesites of UQ but also with their home university. We also found that UQ students in general found resources for course selection [unintuitive to access](../Interviews/Iteration_1/Laura/Laura_2026_03_26_4.md).

In terms of time taken we found that UQ students ended up taking a pretty variable amount of time to finalise courses, with some taking as few as [a few hours](../Interviews/Iteration_1/Ranvir/Inverview4.md) and some taking as long as [a month](../Interviews/Iteration_1/Kayla/Kayla_20260325_1.md) in some cases. This lead us to conclude that while the majority of interviewees reported finding course selection difficult or time consuming, the extent to which time was consumed (the amount of time taken for course selection) was much more variable than how much and how students found the process difficult. 
**DECISIONS AND ACTIONS**
Therefore we will:
Based on these findings we will be focusing on specifically on the identified pain points of elective choice and the difficulty students found in finding course information during our product development. If we can specifically focus on these two areas of difficulty identified in our research we believe we can create a valuable product for UQ students. Furthermore while we found the pain points and extent of the problem with regards to course selection being time consuming to be too variable to properly target we hope that by addressing the specific difficulties we identified we will be able to alleviate some of the time  consuming aspects of course selection as a byproduct of making the process easier.
